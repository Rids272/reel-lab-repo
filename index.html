Telling stories

From idea to storyboard

AI clips are only 5–10 seconds long. A 30-second reel is just 4–6 short scenes that tell one story.

The 3-part reel shape

PartTimeJob
Hook0–3sStop the scroll: surprising visual, motion, or a question on screen. Viewers decide in about 1–2 seconds.
Build3–25sThe journey. Change the shot every 2–4 seconds. Add a mini-surprise around 15s.
Payofflast 5sTwist, reveal, smile, or a loop back to the start so people rewatch.
⏱ Best length to start: 15–35 seconds. A 30s reel that people watch to the end beats a 60s one they leave halfway.

Hook formulas

  • Start mid-action: open on the most dramatic moment, then show how it got there.
  • "What if…": "What if cats ran a chai stall?"
  • Impossible visual: a whale swimming through clouds above Mumbai.
  • Before / after: show the ending first, then rewind.
  • Curiosity text: "Wait for the end 😳" or "Part 1 of a story nobody expected".
🚫 Never start with an intro card, a logo, or a slow wide shot. Those cause the biggest drop-off.

Don't repeat yourself: shot variety

Use at least 3 different shot types in every reel:

🏔 Establishing

Wide shot that shows where we are.

🙂 Close-up

Face or emotion. Makes people care.

✋ Detail

Hands, objects, textures.

👀 POV / reaction

What the character sees, or how they react.

🎢 Movement

Tracking, drone or orbit for energy.

✨ Reveal

Camera pulls back or turns to show the surprise.

🤖 Storyboard helper prompt

Paste this into Claude or Gemini and change the idea on the first line:

My reel idea: [a lonely robot finds a flower in a desert city]

Act as a short-form video director. Create a storyboard for a 30-second vertical (9:16) Instagram Reel / YouTube Short made with AI video clips of 8 seconds or less.

Give me:
1. A one-line HOOK for the first 2 seconds.
2. A CHARACTER DESCRIPTION I can reuse word-for-word in every prompt (look, clothes, colours).
3. 5 scenes. For each: shot type, what happens, camera movement, and the full video prompt (subject, action, setting, camera, style/lighting, audio).
4. A voiceover script under 75 words (optional).
5. On-screen text for the hook and the ending.
Use a different shot type in each scene so it doesn't feel repetitive, and end with a twist or a loop.

Keeping characters the same

  • Character first: make the character image before any scenes, and lock their look.
  • Character sheet: in Nano Banana, ask for front, side, close-up and full body on one plain background. Always edit from the original image, not the last edit, or the face slowly drifts.
  • Reuse the same description: copy the exact character text into every prompt.
  • Use reference features: Flow's Ingredients to Video or Frames to Video, or Kling's start/end frame.
  • Hand-over-hand: grab the last frame of a clip and use it as the first frame of the next one for smooth continuity.
Editing

Turning clips into a reel

The edit, step by step

  • Project 9:16 (1080×1920). Import clips in storyboard order.
  • Trim hard. Cut the slow first and last half-second of every clip. AI clips often start and end sluggishly.
  • Cut on motion. Join two clips while something is moving, so the cut hides itself.
  • Voice first, then music. Keep music low (about 15–25% volume) under the voice.
  • Cut to the beat. Place cuts on the music's beats for an energetic feel.
  • Captions. Big, high-contrast, 2–4 words at a time. Many people watch on mute.
  • Safe zone. Keep text away from the bottom 20% and the right edge, where the buttons sit.
  • Fix weird AI moments. Melting hands or a glitchy face? Cut it or re-generate that clip.
  • Export 1080p, 30fps, without another app's watermark.

Before you export: quick check

  • Is something interesting happening in the very first second?
  • Do any 2 clips in a row look the same? Change one.
  • Did I watch it once with sound off, and does it still make sense?
  • Is the ending satisfying, or does it loop?
Going live

Uploading like a pro

📸 Instagram Reels checklist

  • Write a caption with real keywords (what's in the video). Instagram search reads it.
  • Use 3–5 specific hashtags (5 is the maximum now). Hashtags help search, not reach.
  • Pick a cover with a clear subject and a few words.
  • Turn on the AI label ("AI info") for AI-made content.
  • Add trending audio, or your own sound.
  • Reply to comments in the first hour.

▶️ YouTube Shorts checklist

  • Title: short and curious, with one keyword, e.g. "The Robot Who Found the Last Flower 🌼".
  • Description: 1–2 lines and 2–3 hashtags.
  • Answer "Altered or synthetic content" honestly. Tick it for realistic AI scenes.
  • Shorts can be up to 3 minutes, but 15–35s usually gets watched to the end most.
  • Add it to a playlist, and pin a comment that asks a question.

🕖 When to post (India)

Studies point to weekday evenings around 7–9 PM IST, with Saturday evening also strong. Treat this as a starting point. After about 10 posts, your own Insights and YouTube Studio data will show your audience's best time.

Stay safe

Rules that protect your account

✅ Do

  • Label AI content. Both platforms ask for it. On Instagram, accounts featuring AI-generated people without the label get their reach limited.
  • Be original. Instagram stops recommending accounts that mostly repost, and YouTube won't monetize repetitive, mass-produced videos. AI itself is allowed.
  • Make each reel feel different: new story, new shots, your own twist.

❌ Don't

  • Make realistic fake videos of real people, celebrities or politicians.
  • Make fake news or "real event" videos that could fool people.
  • Use cartoon or movie characters, brand logos, or copyrighted songs.
  • Post 20 near-identical videos from one template.
